    Signs of storm damage

    What hail and wind actually do to a roof, and what each one tends to look like.

    Hail

    • Soft, dark bruises where granules have been knocked loose
    • Loose granules collecting in the gutters
    • Dents in soft metal — vent caps, flashing, gutters
    • Shingles split or cracked right at the impact point

    Hail damage usually isn't visible from the ground. It takes someone on the roof to confirm it.

    Wind

    • Shingles lifted at the edge or creased across the middle
    • Shingles missing outright, with underlayment showing through
    • Dents or gouges from blown debris
    • A leak that shows up days after the storm, not during it

    Wind damage spreads. One lifted shingle exposes the row next to it, and that keeps going if it isn't caught.

    Signs a roof is simply worn out

    Not every roof problem starts with a storm. Most roofs just reach the end of what the shingle is built to do.

    • Age at or past what the shingle type is rated for
    • Edges curling up or cupping down across whole slopes
    • Bald patches where granules have worn off down to the mat
    • Decking that feels soft or sags between the rafters
    • Daylight visible through the roof boards in the attic
    • The same spot patched more than once

    A worn-out roof isn't an insurance claim — it's a planned replacement. Knowing which one is actually going on changes everything about how to move forward.

    How the repair-or-replace call gets made

    It comes down to a few specific things, not a guess from the driveway.

    • How much of the roof is actually affected
    • How old the roof is
    • Whether the decking underneath is still sound
    • Whether shingles matching the existing roof are still available

    Repair is often the right answer, not replacement — a properly repaired section can hold up for years. Anyone who quotes a full tear-off without ever climbing onto the roof is guessing, not inspecting.

    What to do after a storm

    Five steps, in order.

    1. 01

      Photograph what's visible from the ground. Don't climb up for a closer look.

    2. 02

      Call for a tarp instead of getting on the roof yourself.

    3. 03

      Get an inspection before filing, so the claim reflects what's actually there.

    4. 04

      File the claim with the real damage documented.

    5. 05

      Have the contractor on site when the adjuster comes out.

    Deadlines and what's covered vary by policy — check the specifics with the carrier directly.
